Arcade Studio — executive rebuild brief

Owner and mandate

Personal-domain continuation of Mike's basement arcade project. A production/business case is a scenario for this personally owned concept, not authorization to create a company, publish, contact vendors, buy parts or move payload into another domain. Specialists are AI advisory/implementation workers, not retained credentialed human consultants.

Mike rejected the previous output as shallow and visually poor. Required outcomes: 1. Arcade/video-game-design advisor reviews the actual proposal and fixes its substance. 2. Business/product-marketing advisor reviews and fixes positioning and executive presentation. 3. Show finished-product concept visuals and the player/game experience, not just cabinet rectangles. 4. Present the general product and production business plan in executive framing. 5. Distribute independent work in parallel; one integration owner and evidence-based acceptance.

Audience, job and recommendation to test

Audience: Mike as prospective owner/founder deciding whether to fund a compelling prototype and later a small production pilot. Job: decide the product, fidelity, spend and evidence needed next. Do not sell a fictitious finished product.

Working concept name: AFTER HOURS / Arcade Studio (private working label; not trademark-cleared). Initial hypothesis: two complementary furniture/control stations create more authentic play and better serviceability than one compromised universal cabinet, without four commercial shells. Advisors must challenge this, not simply agree. Four targets remain Golden Tee, Big Buck Hunter, Silver Strike and Jurassic Park Arcade. Exact releases remain unselected; do not silently replace them.

Commercial proposal must be separate from personal one-off construction. Hardware-only sales, authorized donor integration and a licensed turnkey product have different legal, support and economic models. Pick a plausible initial offer and explain what customers do NOT receive. No packaged unlicensed game binaries, artwork, keys, dongle bypass or false affiliation.

Evidence already available

Read relevant sections, not every file wholesale: - ../PLAN.md — current technical plan, not an executive narrative. - ../research/game-feasibility.md — curated primary-source version/control evidence. - ../research/hardware-and-fabrication.md — current prototype infrastructure assumptions and observed supplier prices. - ../research/rea-workflow.md — actual REA tool smoke is NOT a game test. - ../cad/parameters.json — baseline nominal geometry. - The project owner's private verification record — what was exercised versus not (not included in this public package).

Known source-backed constraints: early Golden Tee Fore and Eagle-era Big Buck are emulation candidates; Silver Strike has no proven open-source runtime in this work; Raw Thrills Jurassic 2015 uses mounted potentiometer guns and separate original I/O/security hardware. Older Sega Jurassic titles are different games. Source flags do not prove speed or a lawful commercial bundle.

Show the virtual golfer, bowler, weapon/target or encounter that makes the play moment recognizable. A golfer uses a club in the game; the person outside the screen uses the trackball. Gameplay-first visual art direction

Mike rejected the environment-only visuals/modern/*-v2.webp studies: no credible golfer taking a tee shot, weak animal models and too little connection between the images and the actual game. Re-exporting those scenes at a larger resolution is not an acceptable response.

Selected route and scope

Mike selected modern-game reference boards rather than AI-generated art. Use inspected, credited official screenshots or trailer excerpts from named commercial games. Do not enable an image-generation provider, incur generation spend, purchase games, or extract/distribute game software for this release. The references illustrate desired visual fidelity and useful camera/action framing. They do not replace the requested arcade titles or prove control compatibility, ownership of the pictured artwork, or a working remaster.

Five directions considered

  1. Environment portfolio: beautiful courses and forests; rejected because it omits the player and the action.
  2. Cinematic trailer board: dramatic establishing shots; useful for mood, insufficient for showing what a guest actually does.
  3. Hardware demonstration: hands on real controllers; useful for ergonomics, but not a substitute for in-game screen views.
  4. HUD-only specification: precise information hierarchy; useful to engineers, insufficient as an image-quality benchmark.
  5. Gameplay-moment board — selected: recognizable player/target/action in real game imagery, paired with the physical input, camera, screen information, feedback and next decision. It answers both of Mike's criticisms directly.

Reading order and source separation

Lead each guide with its strongest gameplay benchmark, then the interactive arcade walkthrough, then the original arcade references and engineering limits. Use the existing restrained dark/amber presentation; let the real game images carry the visual richness. Keep readable captions outside the image, preserve the source HUD and aspect ratio, and allow full-image enlargement.

  • Modern-game benchmark: name the actual title and publisher prominently. Do not label it as footage of Golden Tee, Big Buck Hunter, Silver Strike or Jurassic Park unless it really is that game.
  • Original arcade reference: retain the selected legacy release's own screenshots, controls and source caveats in a separate gallery.
  • Proposed presentation: walkthrough camera/feedback suggestions are design choices, not features proved in an emulator or the selected legacy build.
  • Working runtime evidence: requires an actual documented run. None is supplied by a stock screenshot, source manual or art board.

The canonical action sequences live once in each guide's gameplayStoryboard: six Golden Tee moments and five for each other game. Match a reference to a stage only when the pictured action supports that association. A menu is not a shot; a poised golfer is not ball flight. Different source screenshots may show different holes or sessions; never imply an unbroken captured round.

Image acceptance

  1. Gameplay relevance: Golden Tee leads with a visible golfer at address or mid-swing, not an empty course. Bowling shows a bowler/release or visible ball/pin/result state. Hunting shows an identifiable animal and targeting context. Dinosaur imagery shows a creature encounter, not empty jungle.
  2. Visible detail: mature human/animal models, convincing surfaces and light, readable scene depth. No reskinned primitive or resolution-only improvement.
  3. Honest camera/action claim: describe what is actually in the still. Label promotional imagery, previews and trailer frames accurately. A source game's scope, walking, reload or management UI does not establish an arcade control requirement.
  4. Correct player distinction: a virtual avatar may be visible in sports games; a first-person target view need not show a full person. The physical arcade trackball/gun does not have to appear inside the game-world screenshot.
  5. Provenance: exact source page and asset URL, title, publisher/developer, release context, retrieval date, dimensions and processing. Preserve any watermark. Attribution is not a commercial-use license.
  6. Coherent board: use distinct, useful frames at a consistent visual standard; no near-duplicate crops presented as separate gameplay states.
  7. Presentation proof: inspect the actual image, then the rendered desktop and mobile page. Exercise gallery selection, enlargement, walkthrough tabs, keyboard operation and the links between a stage and its related reference.
