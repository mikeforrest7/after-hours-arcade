Executive judgment

Lead with the room people want to use. Make the acquisition and business choices earn their way into that room.

The earlier package had useful engineering substance but the wrong front door: dimensioned geometry and a technical plan instead of a finished product, a convincing play experience and a decision-ready business case. It also omitted material licensed home alternatives. A redesigned page alone would not fix that omission.

The corrected proposition is a furniture-quality home arcade with full-size controls, a coherent room and a guest experience that does not require a technician. Two custom stations are the design concept, not an assertion that complete factory cabinets fit inside those stations. Buying one or more factory products may change the layout. Do not preserve the drawing at the expense of the best acquisition decision.

Commercial judgment: prototype, not batch launch. A defined hardware kit can be costed; a four-game software bundle cannot be assumed. Neither customer demand nor differentiated value has been demonstrated. The production model tests contribution, tooling, liquidity and cost stress rather than inventing a large market.

2. Positioning and product promise

The personal product

Buyer: Mike, building a basement arcade around four named game experiences. Exact releases, room dimensions, access route and spending ceiling remain owner inputs.

Promise: full-size tactile controls, an intentional room, accessible servicing and a clear guest session. The custom design pursues Golden Tee, Big Buck Hunter, Silver Strike and Jurassic Park Arcade without quietly replacing a requested game with a cheaper namesake.

Proof required: selected title/build, lawful acquisition, the right control family and a complete play session on the actual hardware. Original screen illustrations and rendered furniture are design intent—not compatibility evidence.

Acquisition principle: buy proven displays and control components; fabricate the enclosure where it adds fit or quality; compare a complete licensed cabinet before taking on emulator or donor integration. A factory cabinet is an alternative product with its own footprint and terms, not automatically a rehouseable kit.

The conditional commercial product

The numeric base case is one trackball furniture/control kit, not the whole two-station room. Its scope must clearly exclude the TV, PC and game software. A separate donor-integration service would quote the customer's exact hardware, inspection work, responsibilities and support limits. A licensed turnkey offer requires actual rights and distribution terms before pricing it as a real product.

The value hypothesis is room fit, serviceability, full-size controls and finish—not unauthorized bundled games or an unsupported promise to undercut every factory cabinet. Component certifications do not certify the finished product; liability and applicable safety rules remain production gates.

Thirty-second pitch

A tiny putt, a clean strike, a fast hunting round and a shared jungle adventure: four reasons to gather around two well-designed stations. Buy proven parts and screens; make the furniture and integration distinctive. Compare complete licensed products before committing to custom hardware. Prototype the personal room first; make the production business earn its launch.

4. Market evidence that changes the decision

The authoritative dated competitor records and URLs are in narrative.json, rendered in the presentation. Important conclusions:

  • Golden Tee: Fore! Remastered is a listed factory preorder with the Fore! Complete course catalogue and a commercial-grade trackball. Its official page contains conflicting shipping estimates. Arcade1Up's cheaper Golden Tee 3D Deluxe is a different title collection and was directly listed as sold out. Price is not stock.
  • Silver Strike: League Night Home Edition is a portrait-format reboot. Its manufacturer requires a customer-supplied 4K TV; the free stand is not a free monitor. Arcade Collection is another factory path to Silver Strike, but the GO and full-size configurations differ. Its golf game is PowerPutt, not Golden Tee.
  • Big Buck Hunter: Arcade1Up Pro/World-family cabinets are genuine licensed consumer alternatives because the owner's desired generation is not selected. They do not establish support for the separate Eagle-era candidates. The inspected Mossy Oak URL redirects; a cached sold-out snippet does not prove live stock.
  • Jurassic Park Arcade: the inspected 2015 sources establish a large commercial environmental product and mounted positional controls. No consumer-sized equivalent was established in this bounded research. That is not proof that no home option exists for any Jurassic game, nor does it erase the legitimate commercial-cabinet or donor choices.

Do not compare the custom infrastructure allowance with complete licensed products as though the scopes match. The custom allowance excludes major game-specific acquisition and integration costs; some factory base prices exclude displays. The production model is yet another scope: a hardware-only kit with paid labor and selling/support costs.

5. Business evaluation—not a deferred question

Competitive pressure. A furniture/control kit near a complete licensed cabinet's price needs a specific reason to exist. A coherent finish is an aesthetic proposition, not demonstrated willingness to pay. Alternatives include factory home cabinets, used commercial equipment, existing custom builders, DIY kits and doing nothing.

Economic discipline. Contribution must include paid production labor, landed components, packaging, outbound delivery, acquisition, payment fees and a support/returns allowance. Tooling and startup costs must be recovered separately without double-counting. Customer deposits create performance and refund obligations; they are not evidence of zero cash risk.

Operational discipline. Batch size is a manufacturing choice, not annual demand. A solo builder's capacity, time spent troubleshooting customer hardware and the cost of replacement parts may limit the business before cutting efficiency does. The manufacturing plan carries these constraints and a no-launch recommendation until the gates pass.

Potential narrow entry. A documented trackball kit is more repeatable than a promise to integrate any donor. A bespoke service may command a higher fee for a specific, difficult integration but is not an uncontested niche and cannot be treated as a factory-scale SKU. Evaluate both without assuming one is automatically the winner.

6. Proposed demand gates

These thresholds are decision rules for a small pilot, not market-size estimates or measured outcomes. Full test/cost/stop fields live in narrative.json.validationPlan and are rendered in the presentation.

  1. Personal usability: all four engineering gates pass; at least four of five first-time guests start and complete a chosen round without technical intervention after brief onboarding.
  2. Buyer discovery: after approved outreach, interview 20 qualified buyers. Require eight specific relevant unmet needs and five requests for an itemized quote before treating the positioning as promising. Run this alongside prototyping rather than first financing a factory.
  3. Paid pilot: after a real demo and costed offer, quote ten qualified prospects. Over 30 days, require at least three unrelated paid pilot orders at a price with positive stressed contribution. Three orders do not justify 50 units; larger batches require corresponding committed demand and proven fulfillment/support.
  4. Reseller alternative: approved vendor outreach must produce written eligibility, margin, minimum-order, modification and warranty terms before entering the model as an available business route.

No public posts, direct messages, interviews, deposits, advertisements or vendor contacts have been executed. Each outward artifact/target requires fresh approval. The agent may drive approved work; there is no assumption that Mike must personally perform every contact.
