Arcade/game-experience review: ranked defects in the rejected proposal

2026-11-01 · Arcade systems and video-game experience design advisor · Reviewing ../../PLAN.md, ../../research/*.md, ../../cad/parameters.json, ../../engineering-graph.json

Method and evidence boundary

This review reads the existing plan as an engineering/procurement document that never designed an experience. It is thorough on BOM, CAD and risk bookkeeping, and that discipline is preserved, not thrown out. The defects below are about what the plan never describes: what it physically feels like to walk up and play, what each of the four games actually asks of a body and a controller, and where its technical assumptions (latency, control identity, gun geometry) are stated as a single number or a parts choice instead of an engineered budget. No game was launched, no gun was aimed and no trackball was spun in this review; every new claim below cites a primary manufacturer source (manual, product listing or measurement paper), not a blog ranking. I independently searched for factory-built alternatives to the DIY/donor path mid-review and found two consequential ones (D2); that finding also went to ProductionAdvisor and MarketAdvisor directly so it reaches their files without waiting on this one.

Severity: Critical = the delivered thing would not be the requested experience or would misrepresent it to a guest. High = a named technical assumption is unsupported or wrong and will cause a specific, predictable failure. Medium = a real gap that degrades the experience or wastes spend if uncorrected. Low = a precision/naming gap worth fixing before procurement.

Ranked defects

D1 — Critical — No guest/player experience exists anywhere in the plan

Location: PLAN.md §1–§7 entire document; engineering-graph.json tasks. Observed: The plan is organized around parts, CAD, acceptance tests and a dependency graph. It has zero prose describing what a person does, feels or sees from walking up to the furniture through finishing a session. "Acceptance gates" (§7) test isolated subsystems (trackball direction, aim grid, reload cadence) but never assemble them into a lived sequence. Consequence: Mike rejected the prior output as shallow specifically because it reads as a parts catalog, not a product. A reviewer (or a guest) cannot tell from the plan whether starting a game takes one button or a four-menu crawl, whether a new player has to ask "how do I join," or what happens when someone walks away mid-round. This is the root cause of the rejection, not a side issue. Correction: experience-spec.md §4 (guest journey) and §3 (four game rituals) supply the missing sequence end to end, including the awkward moments (a fifth guest arriving mid-game, a dropped controller identity, a service exit).

D3 — High — A single "~50 ms input-to-photon" target is applied to four games with incompatible sensing requirements, with no stage decomposition

Location: PLAN.md §7 acceptance table, row "Speed and latency." Observed: The plan states "Provisional total input-to-photon target ≤50 ms; measure with high-speed video or an instrumented method" as one number for trackball swing games, a static-aim light-gun game and a continuously-tracked rail shooter alike. PLAN.md does specify a measurement method (high-speed video or an instrumented method) — the gap is the single undifferentiated number and the absence of a stage budget showing where the 50 ms is expected to go, not an absent measurement method. Consequence: The number is unfalsifiable as written and, worse, may not be achievable by default hardware without checking. The CHI 2019 paper's own introduction sums its own per-step order-of-magnitude figures (mechanical switch ~20 ms, controller/firmware ~1–20 ms, USB polling wait ~1–10 ms, etc.) to an illustrative 23–70 ms range — this is the paper's analytical model, not an empirically measured result from its 36-device dataset (whose actual LagBox measurements, shown only as distribution plots, deliberately exclude mechanical-switch latency since contacts are triggered electrically, not by a human finger). Still, the breakdown shows the component stages worth bench-measuring on whatever hardware is actually chosen, and the paper separately confirms Windows rounds a device's requested polling interval down to the next power of two (a device asking for 10 ms is typically serviced at 125 Hz / 8 ms, with an OS cap at 32 ms) (Wimmer, Schmid & Bockes, "On the Latency of USB-Connected Input Devices," CHI 2019). Correction: experience-spec.md §5 decomposes the budget by stage and gives differentiated per-game targets (continuous-tracking rail shooter tightest, as a design-priority judgment, not an independently measured fact; discrete-gesture trackball games most tolerant), and states the MAME-vs-libretro runtime choice as an explicit architecture decision rather than an implied one.

D4 — High — Positional vs. optical gun substitution is treated as a parts/budget question, not the identity-defining decision it is for Jurassic Park Arcade

Location: cad/parameters.json stations.shooter_console.removable_future_positional_gun_deck_mm (a floor-space reservation placeholder, not a parts table); PLAN.md §3 "Shooter station," "Mounted branch"; research/hardware-and-fabrication.md "Gun choices and limits." Observed: PLAN.md's prose already treats the mounted branch and the handheld branch as electrically and functionally distinct ("retain original Jurassic guns/I/O or engineer two-axis analog assemblies only after proving their complete input contract... it does not automatically emulate RIO or make original game electronics accept the new device") — PLAN does not reduce this to a cost/bench-proof-status comparison, and cad/parameters.json's "removable future positional gun deck" is an explicitly labelled floor-space placeholder ("not a usable gun deck or structural design"), not a parts-table row. What PLAN's technical/electrical framing never does is translate that distinction into the guest-facing experience consequence: that a mounted gimbal and a free-aim handheld wand are two different physical rituals, not interchangeable inputs for the same game. Consequence: Raw Thrills' own 2015 Jurassic Park Arcade manual documents a gun on a mounted gimbal with two-axis potentiometers, vibration hardware and a protected 5 A fuse per solenoid circuit (https://rawthrills.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/JP-Manual-r11.pdf, p.2/p.19 as already catalogued in research/game-feasibility.md) — a mechanically grounded pivot, independent of ambient light. The manual confirms position sensing and vibration hardware; it does not document a spring-centering/return mechanism specifically, so "resists and springs back" should be treated as a reasonable design hypothesis for a potentiometer gimbal, not a confirmed fact, pending verification against the actual selected hardware. Optical/IR systems (Sinden, GUN4IR, AimTrak, OpenFIRE) are the opposite architecture: a camera watching a screen border or IR markers, free-aim in the hand, bounded by lens field of view, room lighting and line of sight (research/hardware-and-fabrication.md already notes this correctly for the parts-selection question, but the experience consequence is never drawn). Swapping one for the other does not "install a different gun on the same game" — it changes whether the player is operating a mounted mechanism (whatever its actual resistance/return behavior turns out to be) or free-pointing a wand, which is the core kinesthetic memory of a rail shooter. If the positional branch is never proven, the Jurassic Park Arcade experience as requested is not being delivered, independent of whether some rail-shooter game runs on the console. Correction: experience.json.games[jurassic_park_arcade].fidelityRisks and experience-spec.md §6 state this as an identity gate, not a parts choice: an optical-gun build must be presented to Mike as "a different rail shooter," never labelled Jurassic Park Arcade in guest-facing material, until the positional branch is bench-proven or deliberately declined.

D5 — High — Trackball fidelity guidance ("disable pointer acceleration") does not address the actual sensing requirement Golden Tee's swing mechanic needs, and the specified trackball enumerates as a mouse

Location: PLAN.md §3 "Trackball station"; research/hardware-and-fabrication.md "Trackball controls," specifying the Ultimarc U-Trak. Observed: PLAN's acceptance table already requires "20 hard strokes show correct direction and no wrap/reversal/dropout," and research/hardware-and-fabrication.md already says to "prove count direction/range at both gentle and hard strokes" — both catch gross dropout. Neither test decomposes into the two sub-measurements Golden Tee's documented swing mechanic actually needs: Incredible Technologies' own "Golden Tee 3D Golf Tips and Techniques" manual (hosted at Internet Archive: https://archive.org/stream/arcademanual_Golden_Tee_3D_Golf/Golden%20Tee%203D%20Golf_djvu.txt) describes shot power as coming from two separate measurements of the same gesture: the distance of trackball back-swing (its speed is explicitly irrelevant — "the speed of the back swing makes no difference to the shot, it's the amount of back swing that matters") and the velocity of the forward swing, with "over-hitting" a named failure mode when forward velocity is too high. A pass/fail dropout test does not confirm that peak back-swing distance and peak forward-swing velocity are read correctly as two separate values; a trackball/encoder pipeline that undersamples a fast stroke, or that silently clamps/caps delta magnitude, could still pass the dropout test while manufacturing phantom weak/over-hit shots that have nothing to do with player skill. Separately, the specified part itself carries an unaddressed interface risk: Ultimarc's own product page states the U-Trak's "USB Interface supports left/right mouse buttons" — i.e., in its USB mode it enumerates as a mouse HID device, not a dedicated joystick/trackball device (https://www.ultimarc.com/trackballs-and-spinners/trackballs/u-trak/). If the host application does not deliberately handle this, a vigorous swing can move the Windows system cursor itself — onto a second display, into the taskbar, onto a dialog — at a party, in front of guests, which is a materially different failure than "the game felt stiff." PLAN.md never raises this. Correction: experience-spec.md §7 specifies sample-rate/count-loss bench criteria for the swing gesture and a behavioral cursor-containment acceptance test (no visible system-cursor escape, no focus/window loss, no unintended desktop interaction during vigorous play — the specific mitigation mechanism is an implementation choice, not prescribed), and experience.json.games[golden_tee].prototypeTest / .controlContract encode both.

D6 — Medium — Silver Strike's own documented non-16:9 scaling defect is not caught by the plan's generic "don't stretch 4:3" rule, and directly collides with the brief's 43" screen choice

Location: PLAN.md §4 "Display strategy"; BRIEF.md trackball display contract (43-inch). Observed: PLAN.md's general display guidance ("A 4:3 game is pillarboxed; never stretch it to fill 16:9") is correct as a rule but does not catch a title-specific defect. Incredible Technologies' own 2009 Silver Strike kit manual states plainly: "the software is not designed for a true 16:9 aspect ratio. With the proper DIP switch setting HDMI can be used but many of the objects will have a slightly stretched or skewed look. The larger the HDTV the more apparent the stretching becomes." (https://amusement.itsgames.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/silver-strike-bowling-09-installation-manual.pdf). This is not a cable/scaler problem the plan's existing scaler guidance solves — it is the game's own rendering against a larger panel. Consequence: Selecting the brief's 43-inch trackball display for a donor-path Silver Strike without first confirming the exact DIP-switch display mode risks a visibly distorted bowling lane on the single largest screen in that station, on a title whose manufacturer already warns this gets worse with size. Correction: experience-spec.md §9 adds "confirm documented display-mode DIP setting against the selected panel size" as a named calibration-gate item, not an assumed pass.

D7 — Medium — No acoustic or circulation design for two simultaneously active stations

Location: PLAN.md §2 concept table, "Two modular stations" row; cad/parameters.json room_assumptions. Observed: cad/parameters.json already reserves a 600 mm gap between the two stations (stations.shooter_console.x_gap_after_station_a_mm) and PLAN's room survey already names "spectator circulation" as something to measure, but neither figure was ever derived from or checked against actual foot-traffic adequacy, and nothing addresses the acoustic side at all: golf/bowling ambience and commentary overlapping with gunfire, explosion and dinosaur SFX from six feet away, or a trackball swing's forward-reach zone (strike clearance 300 mm per cad/parameters.json) intersecting foot traffic headed to the shooter holsters. Consequence: A loud concurrent-session experience that was never designed is a likely source of guest complaints and of Mike wanting quieter sessions than the plan assumed were free. This is a guest-journey gap, not a BOM gap. Correction: experience-spec.md §2 and §4 name simultaneous-session audio/circulation as a design input (independent volume limits, a stated minimum circulation offset outside each station's strike/reach zone) rather than leaving it implicit in the room plan's dimensions.

D8 — Medium — Multiplayer player-identity handling is tested, never designed

Location: PLAN.md §7 acceptance table, row "Restart/device identity." Observed: The acceptance table requires "Ten cold boots and planned reconnect scenarios retain P1/P2 identity... Missing devices fail visibly, not by controlling another player" as a pass/fail test. Nowhere does the plan say how a guest actually joins, how player order is shown, or what a party host does when a fifth guest wants in mid-round, a controller is dropped, or P2 leaves to get a drink mid-frame. Correction: experience-spec.md §10 and experience.json.journey specify the actual join/identity/drop-out UX per station, consistent with each game's documented turn structure (Golden Tee's Skip-Turn menu feature, confirmed in the Fore! 2004 manual, is the existing mechanism for exactly this case and should be used rather than re-invented).

D9 — Medium — Calibration is modeled as a one-time commissioning task, not a recurring guest-facing ritual

Location: PLAN.md §5 step 9 "Commission"; §7 acceptance rows "Optical aim," "Positional aim." Observed: PLAN.md already tests that calibration/identity survive a cold restart (§7 "Restart/device identity") and already calls for optical-aim testing under actual room lighting and daylight conditions (§7 "Optical aim," research/hardware-and-fabrication.md). What it never designs is a within-session recalibration ritual for a guest-facing party where lighting can drift (more lamps on, daylight change) without any restart occurring, nor a check that a shared panel's overlay still matches the active game after a profile switch — a trackball's practical zero does not drift, but its shared identity across games (Golden Tee vs. Silver Strike button labels on one panel, per research/hardware-and-fabrication.md) must be re-confirmed whenever the overlay changes. Correction: experience-spec.md §9 adds a short session-start guest ritual, distinct from PLAN's existing one-time commissioning and its existing restart-persistence test, rather than replacing either.

D10 — Medium — Two guns are allowanced in the BOM and reserved in CAD before the selected Big Buck Hunter release's simultaneous-play mode is confirmed (DIY path)

Location: PLAN.md §3 "Shooter station," "Legacy Big Buck may use alternating players on a single gun"; research/hardware-and-fabrication.md reconciled BOM, "Complete non-recoil optical gun, allowance | 2." Observed: Nothing has been physically built in this project — PLAN's own BOM/CAD are concept-stage allowances, not purchases or fabricated parts, and PLAN's G0 gate already requires recording the exact selected release before nonreturnable spending. The plan already contains the correct caveat in prose ("Two installed guns do not create a multiplayer mode the game lacks") but the BOM allowance (quantity 2) and the shooter-console CAD reservation (cad/parameters.json holster_count: 2) are sized for two guns ahead of that G0 decision, and the guest-facing visual/journey material was never explicitly gated on the G0 mode outcome the way the BOM caveat implies it should be. Consequence: If the selected Eagle-era release is alternating-only (the feasibility doc flags bbhsc/bbh2sp as requiring secondary-target/reduced-threshold handling, not confirmed simultaneous dual-gun play), a guest expecting a co-op duel gets a turn-based single-gun game with a second decorative holster — a letdown that is purely an experience-design failure, not a hardware failure. Correction: experience.json.games[big_buck_hunter].desiredExperience and .fidelityRisks state the exact mode as a G0-resolved fact to be confirmed before any guest-facing promise of simultaneous play for the DIY/MAME path, and experience-spec.md §3 writes the ritual for both possible modes so neither is accidentally oversold. A factory buy-path (Arcade1Up's Big Buck Hunter Mossy Oak Deluxe Arcade, confirmed via a live Amazon listing as "1 to 4 players"/"Number of Players: 4") narrows but does not resolve this risk by specification — those are capacity claims, not confirmed evidence of a simultaneous-fire mechanism — if Mike accepts the modern Pro/Safari-era titles it bundles instead of the Eagle-era bench candidates; also added to experience.json.games[big_buck_hunter].softwareBranches (IC5).

D11 — Low — "Service/exit" accidental-trigger testing has no matching recovery UX

Location: PLAN.md §7 acceptance table, row "Buttons" ("Service/exit cannot be triggered accidentally by ordinary play"). Observed: The plan tests that service/exit is hard to trigger accidentally, which is right, but never states what happens on the much more common failure: a guest or a power blip interrupts a round anyway. There is no described resume/restart behavior. Correction: experience-spec.md §11 "Recovery" defines a clean, boring default (full restart to the game's own title screen, not a crash to desktop or a frozen frame) for every station, consistent with PLAN's existing "normal OS shutdown button" discipline.

D12 — Low — No staged, demo-able proof sequence tied to Mike's funding decision

Location: PLAN.md §7 acceptance gates (good engineering content) and §6 wave table. Observed: The acceptance table is a solid bench/QA checklist, but it is not sequenced as a confidence-building demo path for a non-engineer funder deciding whether to keep paying for the next stage. "All four game identities: exact selected build shown" sits in the same flat table as thermal/endurance and safety checks, with no explicit "this is the smallest thing that would convince you the concept works" milestone. Correction: experience-spec.md §12 "Stage-gated prototype proof" reorders the same underlying tests (PLAN's acceptance criteria are not replaced, just resequenced and narrated) into bench → one-game vertical slice → two-station integration → full room acceptance, each stage ending in something Mike can personally watch and approve or reject.

IC1 — Golden Tee's Left/Right buttons do not "merely" fight crosswind; the earlier draft over-generalized a single-generation manual example

Location: experience-spec.md §3.1 (prior draft), experience.json.games[golden_tee].controlContract (prior draft). Observed: The earlier draft sourced Left/Right's function entirely from the 1995 "Golden Tee 3D Golf Tips and Techniques" manual's crosswind/reset hints and generalized that into an absolute claim ("not a parallel aim axis," "not a general aim control") — while the plan actually recommends the later Fore! generation (gtfore06), whose own manual does not describe Left/Right's function at all. Consequence: The manufacturer's own current official gameplay guide (https://livewire.itsgames.com/gt/how-to-play, observed 2026-11-01) states plainly that Left/Right buttons "rotate your golfer 11° in that direction. Golfers can be rotated 360° from any ball location" — i.e. Left/Right are a real shot-direction/setup control, not merely a crosswind countermeasure. The earlier draft's absolute "cannot set shot direction" framing was itself an unsupported claim, not a correction of one. Correction: experience-spec.md §3.1 steps 1 and 6a, and experience.json.games[golden_tee].physicalActions/.controlContract, now describe both the trackball gesture (which shapes shot shape) and Left/Right (which rotates the golfer / sets shot direction) accurately, citing the manufacturer's current guide (new source J19) and flagging the exact degree-per-press/hold-to-rotate behavior as generation-dependent.

IC2 — The CHI 2019 paper's illustrative range was mischaracterized as a measured result

Location: experience-spec.md §5, §15 (J6), prior draft; experience.json.architectureDecisions[AD1], .sources[J6], prior draft; D3 Consequence above. Observed: The earlier draft stated "measured button-press latency before host visibility ranged roughly 23–70ms" as if this were an empirical result from the paper's 36-device study. Consequence: Inspecting the actual paper (https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/40182/1/On_the_Latency_of_USB-Connected_Input_Devices_author_version.pdf) shows the 23–70ms figure appears in the paper's introduction as a sum of its own listed approximate per-step latencies (mechanical switch ~20ms, controller ~1-20ms, USB polling ~1-10ms, etc.) — an illustrative analytical model, stated before the paper describes its actual LagBox measurement method. The paper's real empirical results (§6, Figures 6-8) are device-specific distribution plots, not a single textual range, and explicitly exclude mechanical-switch latency because LagBox triggers switch contacts electrically rather than via a human finger. Correction: All three owned files now describe 23-70ms as the paper's own illustrative per-stage model range, not a measured number, and note that the paper's real device measurements exclude mechanical latency by design.

IC3 — An invented "same seed" fairness mechanic for Big Buck Hunter's alternating mode

What must change in product design, not just what is unknown

  1. Write the guest journey and four play rituals before any more CAD or BOM work — the furniture dimensions in cad/parameters.json already assume a standing swing stance and a two-holster duel that the experience layer never actually specified; CAD is currently ahead of the design it is supposed to express.
  2. Treat "Golden Tee," "Silver Strike" and "Big Buck Hunter" sourcing as an open fork (build vs. buy vs. hybrid), not a settled DIY commitment — this changes furniture count, footprint, and whether Station A is custom at all. Jurassic Park Arcade is the one game confirmed to have no factory alternative in this research.
  3. Replace the single 50 ms latency target with a decomposed, per-game budget and name the actual runtime stack (standalone MAME has no run-ahead; a libretro frontend does, but is a different compatibility/BIOS/performance proposition that has not been evaluated).
  4. Stop describing positional-vs-optical guns as a cost tier for Jurassic Park Arcade; state plainly that an unproven positional branch means the named game is not what gets delivered.
  5. Add a behavioral cursor-containment acceptance test and a swing-gesture sampling test for the trackball station; the currently specified part's USB mode is a mouse, not a dedicated controller, by the manufacturer's own description, so the specific mitigation mechanism (raw input plus confinement, exclusive capture, or cursor hiding) is an implementation choice, not something to assume works by default.
  6. Confirm Silver Strike's documented display-mode DIP setting against the selected panel size before committing to the 43-inch screen for that title.
